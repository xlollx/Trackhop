# Spotify's "Import your music", and what it does not do

Since November 2025 Spotify has a built-in **Import your music** option (run by TuneMyMusic), and
YouTube Music and Apple Music have import tools of their own. They are handy for moving **into**
that one service, once. Trackhop covers what they leave out:

| | Built-in import tools | Trackhop |
|---|---|---|
| Direction | Only into the service that offers it | Any service to any service, Spotify included as a source |
| After the move | A one-off copy | Optional **sync**: by hand or on a schedule, removals too |
| Your own servers | No | Navidrome, Jellyfin, Plex, Subsonic |
| Tracks not found | Skipped | A review list with suggestions, remembered for next time |
| Files | No | CSV, M3U, XSPF and more, as a backup or a bridge |
| Cost | Free, inside one app | Free, open source (AGPL-3.0), no account |

## When the built-in tool is enough

A single move into Spotify (or YouTube Music, or Apple Music), with nothing to keep in step
afterwards: use it, it is right there.

## When Trackhop helps

- **Leaving Spotify**: [to YouTube Music](spotify-to-youtube-music), [Apple Music](spotify-to-apple-music),
  [TIDAL](spotify-to-tidal), [Deezer](spotify-to-deezer), [Amazon Music](spotify-to-amazon-music).
- **Using two services at once** and wanting the same playlists on both: [keep playlists in sync](sync-playlists).
- **Your own music server** next to a streaming service: [Navidrome, Jellyfin or Plex](spotify-to-navidrome-jellyfin).
