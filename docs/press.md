## In three lines

Trackhop keeps playlists identical across music services: Spotify, YouTube Music, Apple Music, TIDAL,
Deezer, Amazon Music and your own Navidrome, Jellyfin or Plex server. Pick a playlist, pick where it
should go, and Trackhop copies it once or keeps it in sync on a schedule. It is free and open source,
with no account, no subscription and no server in between: everything runs on your own device.

## What makes it different

- **Sync, not just transfer.** Most tools move a playlist once. Trackhop keeps watching it: new songs
  reach the other service by themselves, hourly, daily or weekly, and removals can follow too.
- **Free, with nothing locked.** No track or playlist limits, no paid plan, no ads.
- **Private by design.** There is no Trackhop account and no Trackhop server. Logins stay encrypted on
  the phone or computer; nothing about your music is sent to the developer.
- **Open source** under the AGPL-3.0, built in public on GitHub; every download is checked on
  VirusTotal ([details](/guide/security)).
- **Everywhere you are.** An Android app, Trackhop Desktop for Windows, macOS and Linux, and a Docker
  image with a web page for a NAS or home server.
- **Extras around your playlists**: a review of the songs that were not found with one-tap
  suggestions, backups, duplicate removal, a "Wrapped" of your library at any time, playlist
  compatibility between two people, and twelve languages.

The Google Play, AppGallery and Microsoft Store versions use each service's official API only. The
GitHub and F-Droid versions also reach some services through their own web players; these are not
official interfaces, and the app says so before the first sign-in.

Trackhop is not affiliated with any music service; their names are used only to indicate
compatibility.
